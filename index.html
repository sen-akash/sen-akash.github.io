About

I am a PhD scholar in the Department of Mathematics at the Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad, supervised by Prof. C. S. Sastry. My research is supported by the Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF).

My interests lie in the mathematical foundations of deep learning, algorithm unrolling, inverse problems and optimization theory. My doctoral work develops deep unrolled networks: interpretable neural architectures that I design, analyse theoretically and apply to sparse signal recovery in data-driven settings. I aim to connect optimization theory with modern deep learning for inverse problems and signal processing.

I have presented at ICASSP 2024 and 2025, SampTA 2025 and PReMI 2025. My work has appeared in IEEE Signal Processing Letters (2026), with further manuscripts under review at Neural Networks and Applied Soft Computing.

Thesis

Unrolled data-driven solvers for inverse problems

I turn iterative optimization algorithms into trainable networks whose layers keep the structure of the original method. This keeps the networks interpretable and makes them open to theoretical analysis.

Method

Nonnegative least squares and sparse recovery

Several of my papers unroll proximal gradient descent, gradient descent and thresholding schemes into learned networks for nonnegative least squares and nonnegative sparse signal recovery.

Applications

Tomography and impulsive noise

I apply learned networks to electrical impedance tomography, together with S. Gulati and P. Jampana, and to sparse recovery under impulsive noise through LCP-Net.

In preparation

Convex and thresholding-based solvers

Two manuscripts in preparation: a hard thresholding-driven basis pursuit algorithm with momentum under a nonnegativity constraint, and BGST, a convex solver for group sparse recovery with a box constraint.
